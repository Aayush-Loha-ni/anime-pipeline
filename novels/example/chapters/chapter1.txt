The lamp had gone out three nights running.

Mara kept the log anyway, because the log was the one thing on the cliff that did not lie. At dusk she climbed the stairs, counted the gulls, and wrote down the hour in a careful hand. Below her the sea worried at the rocks with the same patient noise it had made for forty years.

On the fourth evening the light came back on by itself. There was no boat, no signal, no reason for it. Mara wrote the hour down, closed the book, and waited to see whether anyone would come to ask about it.
